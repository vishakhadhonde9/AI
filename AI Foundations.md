# Artificial Intelligence -
- It is a branch of Computer Science that pursues creating the computers or machines as intelligent as human beings.
- Artificial Intelligence is the field of computer science focused on creating systems that can simulate human intelligence by perceiving information, learning from data, reasoning about problems, making decisions, and generating outputs such as predictions, recommendations, or content.

# Problems/Challanges -
### 1. Data Dependency -
- Insufficient data can reduce accuracy.
- Poor-quality data leads to incorrect predictions.
- Collecting and maintaining large datasets is expensive.

### 2. Bias and Fairness Issues -
- AI learns from past data. If the past data contains unfair patterns, the AI may learn those patterns and continue making unfair decisions.

### 3. High Development and Maintenance Cost -
- Creating and maintaining AI systems can be very expensive.
- It requires: Powerful computers, Large datasets, Skilled experts and Continuous updates.

### 4. Privacy and Security Concerns -
- AI systems need large amounts of data to learn and make decisions. This data often includes sensitive personal information.
- If this data is not protected properly, people's privacy can be compromised.

### 5. Cybersecurity Risks -
- AI is a powerful technology that can be used for both good purposes (defense) and bad purposes (attacks).
- Defenders use AI to detect threats, identify malware, and protect systems.
- Attackers use AI to create smarter and more convincing cyberattacks.

# Types of AI -
## 1. Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI) -
- Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI), also called Weak AI, is designed to perform a specific task or a limited set of tasks. It cannot operate beyond its programmed domain.
- ANI is the only type of AI that currently exists in practical applications. These systems are highly efficient within their specific area but lack general reasoning abilities.
- They cannot transfer knowledge from one task to another like humans can.
- They can specialized in one task, works within predefined boundaries, cannot think independently and cannot understand context outside its training.
- **Examples:**
   - Voice assistants like Siri and Google Assistant
   - Recommendation systems used by Netflix
   - Spam email filters
   - Face recognition systems
   - Chatbots

## 2. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)
- Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), also called Strong AI, refers to AI that can understand, learn, reason, and solve problems across multiple domains at a human level.
- AGI would possess the ability to transfer knowledge from one task to another and perform any intellectual activity that a human can perform.
- Unlike ANI, AGI would not require separate training for every new task.
- They have human-level intelligence, learns from experience, adapts to new situations, performs multiple tasks and applies reasoning across domains
- An AGI system could: Drive a car, Learn a new language, Write software, Conduct scientific research, Manage a business without needing separate AI systems for each task.

## 3. Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI) -
- Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI) is a hypothetical AI that surpasses human intelligence in every aspect.
- ASI would be capable of reasoning, creativity, learning, decision-making, and problem-solving at levels beyond the most intelligent humans
- They can exceeds human intelligence, extremely fast learning, superior problem-solving ability, advanced creativity and innovation, autonomous decision-making.
- ASI have capabilities as:
   - Solving complex global problems
   - Discovering new scientific theories
   - Designing advanced technologies
   - Making decisions faster and more accurately than humans
